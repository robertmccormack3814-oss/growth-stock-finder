The investment philosophy

The scanner is designed to find companies where several independent signals agree. It asks: Is the business growing quickly? Is that growth economically valuable? Is the company financially durable? Is the market confirming the story? And is the valuation at least defensible relative to the growth?

Growth + Quality + Financial Strength + Market Confirmation + Sensible Valuation → Candidate

A separate risk engine can veto an otherwise high-scoring stock.

1. Growth — 30 points

Growth receives the largest weight. The scanner wants both strong current growth and evidence that growth has persisted over several years.

Current revenue growth
Measures how quickly sales are expanding now. Roughly 30%+ is excellent; around 5% is the low end of the scoring range.
Current earnings growth
Measures whether profits are expanding with the business. Roughly 35%+ is excellent.
Multi-year revenue CAGR
Uses annual financial statements to measure sustained compound sales growth. Roughly 25% CAGR is excellent.
Multi-year EPS CAGR
Measures growth in earnings per share, helping distinguish genuine shareholder growth from simple expansion. Roughly 30% CAGR is excellent.

2. Business Quality — 25 points

Fast growth is much more valuable when the company earns high returns and converts revenue into cash.

Return on Equity (ROE)
How effectively shareholder equity produces profit. ~25% excellent.
Return on Invested Capital (ROIC)
How effectively operating capital produces after-tax operating earnings. ~20% excellent.
Return on Assets (ROA)
Profitability relative to the asset base. ~12% excellent.
Gross Margin
How much revenue remains after direct costs. ~60% excellent in the generic scoring model.
Operating Margin
Operating profit generated from revenue. ~25% excellent.
Free-Cash-Flow Margin
Free cash flow as a percentage of revenue. ~18% excellent.

The quality section is intended to stop the screener from confusing rapid but uneconomic expansion with superior growth.

3. Momentum — 20 points

The scanner requires market evidence as well as fundamental evidence. Strong businesses can remain poor investments when the market trend is deteriorating.

50-day moving average — receives credit when price is above the 50DMA.
200-day moving average — receives credit when price is above the 200DMA and this is also a mandatory PASS gate.
Six-month return — approximately 35%+ receives maximum scoring credit.
12-month relative strength vs Nasdaq Composite — measures how much the stock has outperformed or underperformed the broader Nasdaq over the same period. Around +25 percentage points is excellent.
Proximity to 52-week high — strength near the high is rewarded rather than automatically treating a fallen stock as a bargain.
Volume confirmation — compares recent 20-day average volume with the longer 100-day average. Rising participation strengthens the momentum score.

4. Balance Sheet — 15 points

This section asks whether the company can support its growth without dangerous financial leverage.

Current ratio
Short-term liquidity. Around 2.0 is excellent in the scoring range.
Debt to equity
Lower is better. Around 0.25 or below is excellent.
Net debt / EBITDA
Lower leverage receives more credit. Around 0.5× is excellent.
Interest coverage
Ability of operating earnings to cover interest. Around 10×+ is excellent.

5. Valuation — 10 points

This is a growth scanner, so valuation is important without dominating the model. The goal is not simply to find the lowest P/E company; it is to avoid paying an indefensible price for growth.

PEG ratio — relates valuation to expected growth. Around 1 is excellent; around 3 is unattractive in the model.
P/E relative to growth — compares the available forward/trailing P/E with the strongest relevant growth measure. A high P/E can therefore be more acceptable when growth is genuinely high.
Free-cash-flow yield — free cash flow divided by market capitalisation. Around 6% receives maximum scoring credit.

The risk engine uses annualised volatility, maximum drawdown, beta, debt/equity, valuation relative to growth and company-size risk. Higher volatility, deeper drawdowns, higher beta, more leverage, excessive valuation and smaller size increase risk.

What must happen for a PASS?

A high score alone is not enough. The stock must pass several gates simultaneously.

1. Overall score
78/100 or higher.
2. Genuine growth
Revenue growth ≥10%, OR earnings growth ≥15%, OR multi-year revenue CAGR ≥10%.
3. Cash/profit test
Positive free cash flow OR a positive profit margin.
4. Long-term trend
Price must be above its 200-day moving average.
5. Risk veto
Risk score must be 65 or lower.
WATCH
A non-PASS company scoring 65–77.9 is retained as a Watch candidate.

The gates prevent one spectacular metric from compensating for major weaknesses elsewhere.

What the ideal signal looks like

An ideal candidate might have ~30% revenue growth, ~40% earnings growth, ~25% multi-year revenue CAGR, ~30% EPS CAGR, ROE above 25%, ROIC above 20%, strong margins and free cash flow, low leverage, strong interest coverage, a share price above both major moving averages, positive relative strength against Nasdaq, price near its 52-week high and a valuation that remains reasonable relative to growth.

The scanner is a ranking and research tool, not a guarantee of future returns. The Performance Ledger exists to test whether these rules actually produce useful forward results. As the ledger grows, the weights and thresholds can be evaluated against real signal performance rather than assumed to be optimal.
